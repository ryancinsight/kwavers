## KW-PHYS-002 — Thermal + mass-transfer coupling runs away under rarefaction (vapor accumulation, ~+16 R0 per cycle) [patch] — done (fixed 2026-11-18)

priority: correctness; needs: none; scope: `update_mass_transfer` in `kwavers-physics` (`acoustics/bubble_dynamics/keller_miksis/thermodynamics/transfer.rs`); milestone: none

- **Outcome:** a bubble driven at 800 kPa, 1 MHz, R0 = 3 µm with thermal effects + mass transfer both on stays within the physical inertial-expansion bound (Rmax/R0 ≲ 10–20, matching the adiabatic reference ~4×), instead of growing linearly to Rmax/R0 ≈ 125 over 8 cycles; `analytical::cavitation::passive_dose::tests::thermal_effects_no_runaway_expansion` passes. The same fix must also hold at the triplet-pulse port's own conditions (0.26 MPa, R0 = 2 µm), where the runaway is +14 R0 per cycle (mean R/R0 1.0 → 140 over 12 cycles) and destroys the 3P phasor structure (thermal-on 3P/1P @ 1.5f0 = 1.39 / 1.38 / 1.01 for R0 = 2 / 3 / 5 µm; 3P/1P @ f0 = 0.53 instead of ≈ 0.01).
- **Driver:** the Keller–Miksis drive-contract fix (Nov 18, 2026; instantaneous-pressure contract, Keller & Miksis 1980, JASA 68(2):628–633) unmasked this defect. The old model double-modulated the drive, so p_inf = p0 + amp·sin²(ωt) ≥ p0 — the bubble never saw rarefaction, never expanded (Rmax/R0 ≈ 1.1 under the old drive at the same conditions), and the test passed vacuously. With the corrected drive the four-way split at (3 µm, 800 kPa, 1 MHz, 8 cycles) is: thermal off / mass off 4.2× (matches the simple adiabatic KM reference, 4.1×); thermal on / mass off 4.0×; thermal off / mass on 4.2×; thermal on / mass on **125.1×** — linear growth ≈ +16 R0 per cycle (13.8, 29.6, 45.5, 61.4, 77.4, 93.3, 109.3), i.e. net vapor accumulation every cycle, not a single-cycle inertial event. The defect is NOT confined to 800 kPa: at the port's conditions (2 µm, 260 kPa, 1.74 MHz, 12 cycles, flat-top drive) thermal-on mean R/R0 per cycle is 1.02, 3.69, 16.40, 30.12, 43.84, 57.57, 71.29, 85.01, 98.74, 112.47, 126.19, 139.92 (≈ +14 R0/cycle), while thermal-off oscillates 1.0–2.4 as a periodic steady state should. The per-step equilibrium clamp in `update_mass_transfer` (caps each Euler step at N_eq = p_sat·V·N_A/(R·T)) does not bound cross-step accumulation: as V grows, N_eq grows with it, so vapor keeps pace with saturation and the internal pressure never falls as V^(−γ). The likely missing physics is a heat-transfer limit on evaporation (the Hertz–Knudsen flux used is the kinetic limit; latent heat cannot be supplied fast enough during rapid expansion) — to be established from the literature (Prosperetti 1991, J. Fluid Mech. 222:587–616; Fyrillas & Szeri 1994, J. Fluid Mech. 277:381–407; Smith & Wang 2022, J. Fluid Mech. 939:A28), not assumed.
- **Acceptance:** the four-way split at (3 µm, 800 kPa, 1 MHz, 8 cycles) gives Rmax/R0 ≲ 20 in all four combinations, with the thermal-on/mass-on case within a factor of ~2 of the adiabatic reference; the existing `thermal_effects_no_runaway_expansion` test passes unmodified; no regression in the passive-dose tests.

- **Fix (2026-11-18, both trees, not yet pushed):** the defect was NOT a missing heat-transfer limit — it was the temperature at which the saturation pressure is evaluated. `update_mass_transfer` evaluated `p_sat` at the bulk gas temperature; during a violent collapse T exceeds the critical temperature of water (647 K), where the saturation curve is clamped at the critical pressure (22.06 MPa, ~10,000× the room-temperature value), so the flux drove EVAPORATION during the very phase when condensation should occur, and the equilibrium clamp inherited the same error. The fix evaluates `p_sat` — and the Hertz–Knudsen denominator — at the liquid temperature at the bubble wall `T_w` = 293.15 K (Kreider et al. 2011 form, Eq. 15), in `transfer.rs` only; the `N_eq` clamp keeps its form with `p_sat(T_w)` in the numerator and the gas temperature in the ideal-gas denominator. No flux cap, no rate limit, no α change (default 0.04 kept; see note below).
- **Literature basis:** `p_sat(T_w)` is the universal standard; `p_sat(T_gas)` is used nowhere. Kreider, Crum, Bailey & Sapozhnikov 2011, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 130:3511–3530, §2B, Eq. (15) — "`T_w` is the liquid temperature at the bubble wall, `p_sat` is the saturated vapor pressure evaluated at `T_w`"; flux set to zero above the critical temperature. Yasui 1997, Phys. Rev. E 56:6750–6760 (evaporation term uses `p_v*`, saturation at the liquid temperature `T_∞`; gas temperature appears only in the condensation-term denominator) and Yasui 1996, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 65:2830–2840 (liquid temperature at the wall varies and `p_sat` follows it). Storey & Szeri 2000, Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A 456:1685–1709, §2(d) — "`P_sat` is the saturation pressure at the temperature of the interface", with the warning that the Hertz–Knudsen law "is only valid for reasonable temperatures and pressures, and … loses validity during the terminal stages of the bubble collapse". Fujikawa & Akamatsu 1980, J. Fluid Mech. 97:481–512 (origin of the non-equilibrium condensation treatment). Hao & Prosperetti 1999, Phys. Fluids 11:2008–2019 (vapour-bubble thermal behaviour; `p_sat` follows the liquid temperature). CORRECTION to the Driver above: "Prosperetti 1991, J. Fluid Mech. 222:587–616" does not exist — no such paper was found; the thermal-model reference is Prosperetti 1977, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 61:17–27 (non-condensing gas; no vapour flux). No standard latent-heat flux cap was found in the literature — the coupling is via the interface energy balance (Kreider et al. 2011, Eq. 20), not a cap — so none was invented.
- **Corrected numbers:** four-way split at (3 µm, 800 kPa, 1 MHz, 8 cycles) after the fix — off/off 4.2×, on/off 4.0×, off/on 4.2×, on/on **4.0×** (was 125.1×). Vapour in the both-on case oscillates at ~1e9 molecules with no secular accumulation (first cycle 7.2e8, last 7.3e8); trapped vapour fraction at cycle end ≈ 15% molar (cf. ~14% in Storey & Szeri 2000). Port conditions (0.26 MPa, R0 = 2 µm, flat-top, cycles 6–10): thermal-on 3P/1P @ 1.5f0 = 0.376 / 0.358 / 0.142 for R0 = 2 / 3 / 5 µm (was 1.387 / 1.375 / 1.010), @ f0 = 0.129 / 0.015 / 0.001 (was 0.53). The f0 = 0.129 at R0 = 2 µm is the period-3 subharmonic response intrinsic to the drive at near-resonant R0 (thermal-off shows the same period-3 pattern), not a thermal artifact.

- **Open (not done here):** the accommodation coefficient default 0.04 is the 1935 Alty value (vacuum evaporation at ~0 °C), ~10× below the mainstream 0.35–0.4 used by Yasui, Storey & Szeri, Kreider and Hegedüs; it is conservative (smaller α = less flux) and was deliberately NOT changed in this fix — raising α toward the mainstream would amplify any remaining flux error, so it needs its own sensitivity check. Documented refinements not implemented: a varying wall temperature (Yasui 1996), the critical-temperature switch-off (`ṅ_v` = 0 for T > T_c, Kreider et al. 2011), the Yasui split evap/cond temperatures, and the diffusion cap on condensation (Kreider et al. 2011, Eqs. 16–17). The temperature update's latent term cools the gas as if evaporation were occurring even when mass transfer is off (pre-existing inconsistency: `latent_temperature_rate` computes a Hertz–Knudsen flux from `p_sat(T_liquid)` regardless of the `use_mass_transfer` flag, while `update_mass_transfer` is a no-op) — the mass-off T_max ≈ 700 K vs both-on ≈ 3200 K at the same collapse is consistent with this, but the attribution is not isolated experimentally; noted, not fixed here.
