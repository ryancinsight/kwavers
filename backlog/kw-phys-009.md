## KW-PHYS-009 — Known duplicates and regime gaps in the bubble-dynamics physics (consolidation backlog) [patch] — todo

priority: maintainability/correctness; needs: none; scope: `kwavers-physics` bubble dynamics (`bjerknes_forces`, `interactions`, `bubbly_medium`, `keller_miksis`, `adaptive_integration`, `epstein_plesset` vs `dissolution`); milestone: none

- **Outcome:** each physical law in the bubble-dynamics area has one canonical implementation that other modules call, and the library's own integrators can produce bubble histories in the inertial-collapse regime the formation physics needs.
- **Driver:** the Nov 21, 2026 library-implementation audit (triplet-pulse mechanisms workstream) found the same laws implemented in several places with divergent forms, and one hard regime gap. None of these were introduced by that workstream; they are recorded here rather than refactored opportunistically.
- **Items:**
  1. **Secondary Bjerknes force exists in three forms.** `bjerknes_forces/secondary.rs` implements the time-averaged harmonic form `(ρ₀ω²/8π)·ΔV₁ΔV₂·cos(φ)/d²`; `interactions.rs::BjerknesForceComputer::secondary` implements the instantaneous Crum (1975) form `F_B(t) = −ρ₀·V̇₁V̇₂/(4πd²)` (plus its own duplicate `BubbleInteractionType`); the `bjerknes_forces/mod.rs` header previously documented a third formula matching neither (header corrected Nov 21, 2026 under KW-PHYS-007; the two implementations remain). Consolidate: one SSOT, the other form expressed in terms of it or removed.
  2. **Primary Bjerknes force exists in three places** (`bjerknes_forces/primary.rs`, `interactions.rs`, CEUS radiation force). Consolidate to one.
  3. **Wood sound speed exists in two places** (`bubbly_medium.rs` — canonical — vs `interactions.rs::CollectiveEffects`). Remove the duplicate.
  4. **The name Epstein–Plesset denotes two different physics**: `bubble_dynamics/epstein_plesset/` is the oscillation-*stability* theorem; `bubble_dynamics/dissolution/` is the Epstein–Plesset *dissolution* law. Rename one (or both) so imports cannot confuse them.
  5. **The library cannot integrate an inertial collapse.** `KellerMiksisModel::calculate_acceleration` returns an error above wall Mach 0.95, and `AdaptiveBubbleIntegrator`'s default stability monitor rejects any state with |Ṙ| > 0.1·c (`MAX_VELOCITY_FRACTION = 0.1`, kwavers-core constants). At the Louisnard & Gomez (2003) Fig. 4 conditions (26.5 kHz, 2 bar, R₀ = 3 µm) the bubble expands to ~102 µm and collapses through both bounds; integration deadlocks with `ConvergenceFailure` (substeps exhausted) even with the monitor disabled, because the Mach guard is inside the equation itself. The KW-PHYS-004 benchmark therefore integrates a test-local transcription of the prototype's Keller–Miksis RHS (which has no such guard). A library intended for cavitation physics needs an integration path through violent collapse — e.g. a guard that degrades gracefully (clamped/regularised compressibility terms) instead of erroring, or a documented stiff-solver path.
     - **Status (Build 22 Part 3, Nov 2026): RESOLVED — local trees only, not yet pushed.** Landed together, per the diagnosis in `B22_PART3_DIAGNOSIS.md`: (i) new implicit SDIRK2 stepper `adaptive_integration::ImplicitBubbleIntegrator` (L-stable, Newton/FD-Jacobian, step-doubling with midpoint certification), documented in code with validation status and validity regime; (ii) Mach guard in `keller_miksis/equation.rs` degraded to a regularised `(1 − Ṙ/c)` coefficient (floor 0.05, sign preserved), with Mach recorded on the state instead of erroring; (iii) the stability monitor shared by both integrators no longer rejects on wall-velocity magnitude. The KW-PHYS-004 benchmark was rewired onto the library path (test-local transcription deleted) and reproduces all six Fig. 4 anchors within the paper's factor-of-2 bands, matching the prototype LSODA law values to ≲0.5% ((3,2) 222.9, (5,2) 94.6, (10,2) 28.2, (20,2) 9.5, (5,1.3) 30.8, (5,5) 275.3 µm/s); trajectory extremes match a properly-sampled LSODA dense-output reference to 0–15% in Rmin and a factor ≲2 in peak |Ṙ|. Two findings recorded in `B22_PART3_BUILD.md`: the diagnosis's LSODA spike extrema (0.023 µm / 6044 m/s) were themselves under-sampled (corrected reference: 0.016 µm / ≈5.3×10⁴ m/s at (5 µm, 5 bar)); and the library equation's documented first-order viscous-derivative approximation (no +4μ/(ρc) LHS term) accounts for the residual peak-|Ṙ| difference at the 2-bar anchors — a modelling decision left for a separate item, not changed here. Acceptance for this item is met as written. Note: the crate's lib-test target does not compile as found (unrelated incomplete `growth/` workstream — missing `growth/tests.rs`, `tracing` imports in `growth/saturation/tests.rs`); the benchmark acceptance was executed through the integration target `crates/kwavers-physics/tests/louisnard_gomez_fig4_benchmark.rs`, and the unit tests were compile-verified against the same pre-existing errors only.
  6. **The fitted influx step is deliberately NOT implemented.** Build 10 of the triplet-pulse formation work (Nov 21, 2026) exhausted the cycle-number mechanism family (shielding, nucleation-limited, gas-supply, resonance, protocol re-read — each ruled out on derived/computed grounds). The influx cutoff that reconciles the Kuji and Iwasaki protocols exists only as a **fitted** phenomenological switch: `N_cut ∈ [21, 23]` cycles, residual fraction `q ∈ [0, 0.01]` (representative N_cut = 23, q = 0 → Kuji 0.88×, Iwasaki 1.04× at C = 0.2). It has no derivation and must not enter the library as a physical law. If a future formation model needs it, implement it in the *model* (not the physics layer), named and documented as fitted with this window stated.
- **Acceptance:** items 1–4 each reduced to one canonical implementation with callers migrated and tests green; item 5 demonstrated by integrating the KW-PHYS-004 Fig. 4 conditions through the library's own KM + integrator; item 6 requires no code — it is a standing decision, revisited only if a derivation appears in the literature.

### Physics review addendum (2026-11-27) — local trees only, not yet pushed

Full report: `~/workspace/kwavers-work/PHYSICS_REVIEW_2026-11-27.md`.

- **Item 5 follow-up — the viscous-derivative approximation is closed.** `estimate_wall_pressure_derivative` no longer drops the R̈ part of d/dt[4μṘ/R]: the Keller–Miksis LHS now carries the prototype's +4μ/(ρc) term, so the library equation matches the prototype's standard form exactly. The same helper also had a second, previously unrecorded inconsistency, fixed in the same pass: under the non-thermal closure it differentiated the full gas pressure including the constant vapour pressure; the derivative now acts on (p_gas − pv), as the library's own Gilmore helper already did (acceptance: inverted dp_net/dt matches the corrected form to 5.4e-14 relative at a pv ≠ 0 state; the pre-fix form deviated 9.8%). At the Fig. 4 anchors pv = 0, so the benchmark is unaffected by the second fix.
- **Item 5 status correction — the trajectory reference was still under-sampled.** The Part 3 status above attributes the 2-bar peak-|Ṙ| residual (factor ≲2) to the viscous-derivative approximation. That attribution is **superseded**: the Part 3 "corrected" LSODA reference sampled dense output on a uniform 4×10⁶-point grid (~9.4 ps spacing) while the |Ṙ| spike is ~ps wide. Spike-resolved re-measurement (solver internal nodes + fs-scale windows; two tolerance levels in exact agreement) gives (3 µm, 2 bar) Rmin 0.0149 µm / max |Ṙ| 29,479 m/s; (5 µm, 2 bar) 0.0297 µm / 20,842 m/s; (5 µm, 5 bar) 0.0160 µm / 54,157 m/s. The post-fix library matches all three to printed precision; the pre-fix library was already within 0.6% — the "residual" was a reference artefact, and the +4μ/(ρc) term's true effect is the last ≤0.6%. `B22_PART3_BUILD.md` §3's 2-bar reference values are retired (historical report not edited; this note and the review report carry the correction). In-code docs and the benchmark's assertion bands were re-centred on the spike-resolved values.
- **Items 1–4 assessed (inventory only; no consolidation, per the review brief).** Item 1: the two secondary-Bjerknes magnitudes agree exactly (the harmonic form is the exact time average of the instantaneous Crum form), but their **sign conventions are opposite** — `bjerknes_forces` documents positive = attractive while `interactions.rs` returns negative for in-phase pairs even though its own `interaction_type` calls in-phase Attraction; a consolidation must fix the convention explicitly, not just deduplicate. Item 2: `bjerknes_forces/primary.rs` and `interactions.rs::primary` are exact duplicates under parameterisation (−V·∇p); the CEUS `calculate_radiation_force` is a different (travelling-wave, time-averaged) specialisation carrying a documented deliberate deviation (radial-amplitude ε in place of ΔV/V₀ ≈ 3ΔR/R₀, ~3× under the Leighton Eq. 3.59 form it cites) retained for a legacy caller contract — disposition at consolidation. Item 3: the two Wood formulas are algebraically identical; the `interactions.rs` copy is unguarded (no β clamp, no non-positive-input fallback) **and its argument order differs** from the canonical signature — a call-site transposition hazard to eliminate at consolidation. Item 4: collision confirmed as recorded; additionally the stability module's header cites "Epstein & Plesset 1953" where J. Chem. Phys. 18 is 1950 (the dissolution module cites 1950 correctly) — trivial doc slip, fix alongside the rename.
- **Guard-soundness fixes landed in the same review** (checked variants and Blake guards; Bader variant was already NaN-tight and is the standard): Louisnard–Gomez checked variant now rejects NaN radius (regime flag and degenerate check were both NaN-blind) and rejects non-positive/NaN diffusivity, saturation concentration, temperature, and ambient pressure — rejects its own doc comment already listed but the body lacked (doc was the intended contract; no production callers relied on the acceptance). ### Consolidation addendum (2026-11-27) — items 1–4 RESOLVED, local trees only, not yet pushed

Full report: `~/workspace/kwavers-work/CONSOLIDATION_2026-11-27.md`.

- **Item 1 — RESOLVED.** Sign determined from the literature: the scalar is the line-of-centres component of the force on one bubble due to the other, positive in the direction away from the other bubble, `F = −(ρ₀/(4πd²))·⟨V̇₁V̇₂⟩` (Crum 1975, *J. Acoust. Soc. Am.* 57(2), 536–538; Leighton 1994 §3.4; Lanoy et al. 2015, Eq. (3) after Crum, whose `(r⃗₁ − r⃗₂)` direction vector states the same result) — in-phase pairs attract (`F < 0`), antiphase pairs repel (`F > 0`). `interactions::BjerknesForceComputer::secondary` already matched this convention; `bjerknes_forces::BjerknesCalculator::secondary_bjerknes_force` was flipped (it returned the attraction-positive strength, contradicting the derivation line in its own doc comment). Both now evaluate one canonical kernel in `bjerknes_forces/secondary.rs` (`secondary_bjerknes_force_instantaneous` / `secondary_bjerknes_force_mean`); the calculator's magnitudes are unchanged, its sign is corrected. Callers of the flipped implementation: in production code only the pass-through wrapper `secondary_bjerknes_force_checked` (`regime.rs`) — its returned `secondary`/`total` values change sign; all other callers were tests, whose sign assertions were updated to the unified convention. The duplicate `BubbleInteractionType` enum in `interactions.rs` is removed; `interaction_type` now returns the canonical `BjerknesInteractionType` (classification behaviour unchanged, including the zero-product → Repulsive edge).
- **Item 2 — RESOLVED.** Canonical `primary_bjerknes_force(volume, pressure_gradient) = −V·∇p` in `bjerknes_forces/primary.rs`; the calculator method (at `V = (4π/3)R³`) and `BjerknesForceComputer::primary` both delegate; valid-input values bit-identical. The CEUS `calculate_radiation_force` stays as a named variant: its doc header now labels it the deliberate CEUS travelling-wave time-averaged magnitude specialisation, cross-referencing the canonical law (the radial-ε vs `ΔV/V₀ ≈ 3ΔR/R₀` deviation is retained for the legacy caller contract, as documented inline).
- **Item 3 — RESOLVED.** `CollectiveEffects::wood_sound_speed` removed (its only caller was its own unit test); the canonical guarded `bubbly_medium::wood_sound_speed` is the sole implementation. A note at the removal site records the removed copy's differing (density/speed-interleaved) argument order, the transposition hazard eliminated.
