## KW-PHYS-009 — Known duplicates and regime gaps in the bubble-dynamics physics (consolidation backlog) [patch] — todo

priority: maintainability/correctness; needs: none; scope: `kwavers-physics` bubble dynamics (`bjerknes_forces`, `interactions`, `bubbly_medium`, `keller_miksis`, `adaptive_integration`, `epstein_plesset` vs `dissolution`); milestone: none

- **Outcome:** each physical law in the bubble-dynamics area has one canonical implementation that other modules call, and the library's own integrators can produce bubble histories in the inertial-collapse regime the formation physics needs.
- **Driver:** the Nov 21, 2026 library-implementation audit (triplet-pulse mechanisms workstream) found the same laws implemented in several places with divergent forms, and one hard regime gap. None of these were introduced by that workstream; they are recorded here rather than refactored opportunistically.
- **Items:**
  1. **Secondary Bjerknes force exists in three forms.** `bjerknes_forces/secondary.rs` implements the time-averaged harmonic form `(ρ₀ω²/8π)·ΔV₁ΔV₂·cos(φ)/d²`; `interactions.rs::BjerknesForceComputer::secondary` implements the instantaneous Crum (1975) form `F_B(t) = −ρ₀·V̇₁V̇₂/(4πd²)` (plus its own duplicate `BubbleInteractionType`); the `bjerknes_forces/mod.rs` header previously documented a third formula matching neither (header corrected Nov 21, 2026 under KW-PHYS-007; the two implementations remain). Consolidate: one SSOT, the other form expressed in terms of it or removed.
  2. **Primary Bjerknes force exists in three places** (`bjerknes_forces/primary.rs`, `interactions.rs`, CEUS radiation force). Consolidate to one.
  3. **Wood sound speed exists in two places** (`bubbly_medium.rs` — canonical — vs `interactions.rs::CollectiveEffects`). Remove the duplicate.
  4. **The name Epstein–Plesset denotes two different physics**: `bubble_dynamics/epstein_plesset/` is the oscillation-*stability* theorem; `bubble_dynamics/dissolution/` is the Epstein–Plesset *dissolution* law. Rename one (or both) so imports cannot confuse them.
  5. **The library cannot integrate an inertial collapse.** `KellerMiksisModel::calculate_acceleration` returns an error above wall Mach 0.95, and `AdaptiveBubbleIntegrator`'s default stability monitor rejects any state with |Ṙ| > 0.1·c (`MAX_VELOCITY_FRACTION = 0.1`, kwavers-core constants). At the Louisnard & Gomez (2003) Fig. 4 conditions (26.5 kHz, 2 bar, R₀ = 3 µm) the bubble expands to ~102 µm and collapses through both bounds; integration deadlocks with `ConvergenceFailure` (substeps exhausted) even with the monitor disabled, because the Mach guard is inside the equation itself. The KW-PHYS-004 benchmark therefore integrates a test-local transcription of the prototype's Keller–Miksis RHS (which has no such guard). A library intended for cavitation physics needs an integration path through violent collapse — e.g. a guard that degrades gracefully (clamped/regularised compressibility terms) instead of erroring, or a documented stiff-solver path.
  6. **The fitted influx step is deliberately NOT implemented.** Build 10 of the triplet-pulse formation work (Nov 21, 2026) exhausted the cycle-number mechanism family (shielding, nucleation-limited, gas-supply, resonance, protocol re-read — each ruled out on derived/computed grounds). The influx cutoff that reconciles the Kuji and Iwasaki protocols exists only as a **fitted** phenomenological switch: `N_cut ∈ [21, 23]` cycles, residual fraction `q ∈ [0, 0.01]` (representative N_cut = 23, q = 0 → Kuji 0.88×, Iwasaki 1.04× at C = 0.2). It has no derivation and must not enter the library as a physical law. If a future formation model needs it, implement it in the *model* (not the physics layer), named and documented as fitted with this window stated.
- **Acceptance:** items 1–4 each reduced to one canonical implementation with callers migrated and tests green; item 5 demonstrated by integrating the KW-PHYS-004 Fig. 4 conditions through the library's own KM + integrator; item 6 requires no code — it is a standing decision, revisited only if a derivation appears in the literature.
